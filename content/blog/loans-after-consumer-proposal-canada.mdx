Yes — you can get **loans after consumer proposal Canada**, and the smartest way to get approved isn't to hunt for a lender who'll overlook your file. It's to read the calendar. Every debt you folded into your proposal is flagged **R7** on your Equifax and TransUnion reports, and that flag runs on a clock that eventually hits zero and disappears. Once you understand exactly when your R7 drops off — and use your **Certificate of Full Performance** to prove you're finished — you can time each application to the moment it's most likely to succeed. Here's the clock, stage by stage, and what to realistically apply for at each point.

## Quick Answer

**Loans after consumer proposal Canada** approvals get easier on a predictable schedule, not a lender's whim. Your proposal marks the included debts **R7** — "paying through a special arrangement to settle the debt" — which is meaningfully softer than the **R9** a bankruptcy leaves behind. That R7 stays on your report for roughly **three years from the date you complete the proposal, or six years from the date you filed it, whichever comes first**, then falls off automatically. The winning move is to match your applications to that timeline: a **secured card** the moment you finish, a **credit-builder loan** as your first clean tradelines report, a **small installment loan** as your score climbs, and **unsecured credit** once the R7 clears. If you're still mid-proposal, the rules are completely different — read our companion guide on [loans during a consumer proposal in Canada](/blog/loans-during-consumer-proposal-canada) before you borrow a cent.

## A Consumer Proposal Is Not Bankruptcy — and Lenders Know It

A consumer proposal is a formal insolvency process under the **Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act (BIA)**, filed only through a **Licensed Insolvency Trustee (LIT)** and overseen by the **Office of the Superintendent of Bankruptcy (OSB)**. You negotiate to repay a portion of what you owe — often far less than the full balance — over a maximum of five years, and unlike bankruptcy you generally **keep your assets**. It is a settlement, not a surrender.

That distinction shows up directly in how lenders read your file. A proposal is reported as **R7**, the rating for someone making regular payments through a special arrangement. Bankruptcy is reported as **R9** — the worst possible rating. To an underwriter, R7 says "this person restructured and paid," while R9 says "this person defaulted entirely." Same fresh start on paper; a noticeably gentler flag in practice.

So drop the idea that a proposal has branded you as un-lendable. It hasn't. What it has done is start a clock — and your job is to work with that clock instead of against it. For a side-by-side sense of how insolvency records age, our guide on [how to get approved after bankruptcy](/resources/guides/how-to-get-approved-after-bankruptcy) covers the harsher R9 path, which makes the R7 route look forgiving by comparison.

## The R7 Clock: When the Flag Actually Drops Off

Here's the part almost nobody explains clearly. Your consumer proposal is governed by **two separate clocks**, and the one that expires *first* is the one that wins.

- **Six years from the filing date.** The countdown starts the day you signed the proposal with your LIT.
- **Three years from the completion date.** The countdown starts the day you make your final payment and the proposal is officially complete.

Whichever milestone arrives first is when the R7 drops off both bureaus automatically. Because a proposal can run up to five years, the arithmetic matters:

| Milestone | What it is | Which clock it starts |
|---|---|---|
| Filing date | The day you signed the proposal with your LIT | Six-year clock begins |
| Completion date | The day of your final payment (Certificate issued) | Three-year clock begins |
| Drop-off | R7 removed from Equifax and TransUnion | Whichever clock ends first |

A quick example. File in year zero, finish paying in year three: the three-year completion clock ends at year six — the same time the filing clock ends, so the record clears at year six. But finish in year two (you paid the proposal off early), and the three-year completion clock ends at year five — a full year *before* the six-year filing clock. Paying early can genuinely pull your clean date forward. Either way, the entry disappears on its own; the bureaus handle it. Equifax's own [credit education library](https://www.consumer.equifax.ca/personal/education/) is worth a read so you know what you're looking at when you pull your file.

## Loans After Consumer Proposal Canada: A Stage-by-Stage Timeline

Knowing the clock is half the strategy. The other half is applying for the *right product at the right stage*, so you're never asking a lender for something the calendar says is premature. Here's the realistic ladder for **loans after consumer proposal Canada**, from the day you finish to a fully clean file.

| Stage | Where you are | Realistic to apply for |
|---|---|---|
| Just completed | Certificate in hand, R7 still showing | Secured credit card |
| ~1 year out | One clean tradeline reporting | Credit-builder loan; small secured line |
| ~2 years out | Two positive tradelines, score climbing | Small reported installment loan |
| 3 years / clean | R7 has dropped off | Unsecured personal loan at fair rates |

![A timeline planner and calendar showing the R7 clock counting down after completing a consumer proposal](https://images.pexels.com/photos/4386292/pexels-photo-4386292.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

### Stage 1 — Just completed: start secured

The day your proposal is done, don't reach for an unsecured loan the R7 will still block. Reach for a **secured credit card** instead. You post a refundable deposit (often $200–$500) that becomes your limit, so the lender takes essentially no risk — which is exactly why it approves a file that still shows R7. Put one small recurring bill on it, pay it in full every month, and you've planted your first fresh, positive tradeline.

### Stage 2 — About a year out: add a credit-builder loan

With a few months of clean history, layer on a **credit-builder loan**. The "loan" sits in a locked savings account while you make fixed payments; you receive the money (minus modest fees) at the end. You're effectively paying to manufacture a spotless installment-payment record — the exact data point a proposal wiped from your file. Two reporting tradelines beat one, and this is the cheapest way to get the second.

### Stage 3 — Around two years: a small reported installment loan

By now your score is climbing and you have real, recent history. This is when a **small installment loan from an alternative or subprime lender** becomes realistic. Rates run higher than a bank's — but no legal loan in Canada can exceed the criminal rate of **35% APR** as of 2025, so treat anything above that as a scam, not an offer. Borrow a modest amount, repay it on schedule, and you add a genuine installment tradeline. Compare offers rather than grabbing the first one; our guide to [bad credit loans](/loans/by-type/bad-credit-loans) shows what a fair deal looks like at this stage.

### Stage 4 — Three years or clean: unsecured at fair rates

Once the R7 has dropped off and you've stacked two or three years of on-time payments, **unsecured personal loans at reasonable rates** come back into range. This is the payoff for playing the clock: instead of begging past a flag, you're applying with a rebuilt file that stands on its own. ## Your Certificate of Full Performance Is the Proof

The single most valuable piece of paper in this whole process is your **Certificate of Full Performance**. Your LIT issues it once you've made every payment and completed the two mandatory financial-counselling sessions. It is the official proof that your proposal is finished — and it's what starts your three-year completion clock.

Two practical moves make it work for you:

1. **Pull both bureau reports and check the completion date.** A proposal that still shows as "active" long after you finished, or a wrong completion date, quietly delays your drop-off. If Equifax or TransUnion has it wrong, your certificate is the evidence you use to correct it. Our primer on [understanding credit reports](/resources/guides/understanding-credit-reports) walks through exactly how to read and dispute the entry.
2. **Keep a copy for lenders.** A subprime lender assessing you between stages will look far more favourably on an applicant who can hand over a Certificate of Full Performance than one who just claims to be "done." It turns your word into a documented fact.

## How to Rebuild Credit Fast After Your Proposal

Timing gets you approved; habits get you a better rate. A simple routine does most of the heavy lifting after your proposal:

- **Keep every payment automatic and on time.** A single 30-day late can erase months of progress. Autopay at least the minimum on everything.
- **Keep utilisation low.** Stay under about 30% of any card limit — ideally under 10%. High balances hurt even when you clear them monthly.
- **Add tradelines slowly.** One clean, reported account does more for a rebuilding file than three rushed applications and their hard inquiries.
- **Watch the clock and the report together.** Check both bureaus every few months so you catch the R7 drop-off when it happens — and can immediately apply a rung higher.

If old collections are lingering alongside the proposal, handle them carefully; our guide on [rebuilding credit after collections](/resources/guides/how-to-rebuild-credit-after-collections) explains how to do it without dinging your score. And for a picture of the wider debt landscape, the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada's [managing debt](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/debt.html) resources are a solid, unbiased starting point.

## The Bottom Line

**Loans after consumer proposal Canada** approvals aren't a matter of finding a lender who'll ignore your history — they're a matter of timing. Your included debts carry an R7 flag that clears roughly three years after you complete the proposal or six years after you filed it, whichever comes first, and a proposal is read far more gently than a bankruptcy's R9. Start secured the day you finish, add a credit-builder loan, step up to a small installment loan as your score rises, and reach for unsecured credit once the clock runs out. Keep your Certificate of Full Performance handy as proof, watch both bureaus for the drop-off, and let time and clean payments do the rest.
