**Can you go to jail for not paying a payday loan Canada** lenders issued? No. Canada abolished imprisonment for debt long ago, and no collection agency has the power to reinstate it over the phone. But there is one genuine exception, it is almost never explained properly, and it has a twist that reverses the usual advice: in the one situation where a Canadian court can order jail in connection with a debt, **paying the debt does not fix it.**

## Quick Answer

You cannot be jailed for owing money on a payday loan. British Columbia's Court Order Enforcement Act puts the principle in seven words at section 51: *"A person must not be taken in execution on a judgment."* The only route to custody runs through **contempt of court** — defying a judge's order to attend a hearing — and that is a punishment for the defiance, not for the debt.

## Can You Go to Jail for Not Paying a Payday Loan Canada Lenders Issue?

| Stage | What it is | Custody risk |
|---|---|---|
| Returned payment | A banking event; fees apply | None |
| Collection contact | Regulated calls and letters | None |
| Credit reporting | A collection entry, usually via an agency | None |
| Lawsuit and judgment | A civil claim in small claims court | None |
| Garnishment or seizure | Civil enforcement of the judgment | None |
| **Ignoring a court order to attend** | **Contempt proceedings** | **This is the exception** |

Every stage before the last one is civil. Nobody is arrested, charged or detained at any point in it.

## Can You Go to Jail for Not Paying a Payday Loan Canada? The Exception

After a creditor wins a judgment, it can ask the court to order you to attend a **judgment debtor examination** — a hearing where you answer questions about your income, your property, and your ability to pay. In Ontario this is started with a Notice of Examination, served on you at least 30 days beforehand, along with a blank Financial Information Form.

If you do not attend, Ontario's guidance is direct: *"If the debtor (or other person) does not attend the examination hearing, the judge may order the person to attend a contempt hearing."* And at that hearing, *"The judge may also make an order that you are to be jailed up to five days for contempt of court."* If such an order is made, the clerk issues a **Warrant of Committal (Form 20J)** directed to the police, authorising them to take the person to the nearest correctional institution for the period specified.

That is the whole of it. Not for owing money — for ignoring the court.

## The Twist: Paying Does Not Undo It

This is the detail that makes the exception worth understanding rather than merely fearing, and it is almost absent from articles on this topic.

Ontario's small claims guidance warns that if you have been ordered to attend a contempt hearing and have not had that order set aside, you **must** attend *"even if, in the meantime, you have paid your debt to the creditor,"* because you were ordered there *"for your behaviour in court… not for failing to pay the debt. Paying the debt does not expunge the contempt."*

So the instinct that solves every other stage — scrape the money together and settle — does not solve this one. The only thing that resolves a contempt problem is dealing with the court: attending, or filing a motion beforehand to have the order rescinded. Anyone asking **can you go to jail for not paying a payday loan Canada** courts oversee should take away exactly one action item from this article: **never ignore a document with a court file number on it.**

## What to Do If You Genuinely Cannot Attend

Since attendance is the whole ballgame, it is worth knowing what to do when the date is impossible — a shift you cannot move, a hospital stay, no childcare.

**Do not simply not show up.** That is precisely the act that creates the risk. Instead:

- **Contact the court office as early as you can.** A request to change a date is ordinary and is far more likely to succeed before the date than after it.
- **File a motion.** Ontario's process allows a debtor to file a Notice of Motion and Supporting Affidavit before the contempt hearing date, asking the judge to rescind the order for a contempt hearing. That option exists precisely for people whose failure to attend was not defiance.
- **Bring the paperwork completed.** For an examination you will have received a blank Financial Information Form. Filling it in beforehand — income, expenses, assets, debts — converts a confrontation into an administrative meeting.
- **Say plainly that you cannot pay, if that is the truth.** An examination exists to establish your means. "I have no means" is a complete and acceptable answer when it is accurate, and it is not contempt.

Being unable to pay has never been the offence. Being absent when a judge ordered you to be present is the only thing that turns this into a question about custody at all.

## When Non-Payment Becomes Criminal

There is a second, genuinely criminal scenario, and it has nothing to do with being unable to repay. Fraud is prosecuted where there was **deception at the time of borrowing** — a false identity, forged or altered pay stubs, fabricated employment, an account that was never yours.

The distinction is the state of mind when you applied, not the outcome. Someone who borrowed honestly and then lost hours, got sick or had a car die has committed no offence, however large the balance grows. Someone who submitted a doctored document to obtain the advance has, whether or not they later repay.

A related point worth stating plainly: a **bounced pre-authorized debit is not a crime.** It is a returned item. It costs money — up to $20 from the lender as a dishonoured-payment fee, and up to $10 from a federally regulated financial institution since 12 March 2026 — but it carries no criminal consequence whatsoever.

## What Collectors May and May Not Say

Because the honest answer to *can you go to jail for not paying a payday loan Canada* is simply "no", collection scripts sometimes drift toward implying otherwise. In Ontario the rules are explicit. A collection agency may not use threatening, profane, intimidating or coercive language, may not give false or misleading information, and may not threaten legal action without the creditor's written authority. It cannot contact you more than three times in seven days after first contact, cannot call between 9 p.m. and 7 a.m., and may only call on Sundays between 1 p.m. and 5 p.m.

Two rights are worth memorising. First, if you **dispute the debt and tell the agency to take the matter to court**, it must stop communicating with you. Second, an agency with ten or more collectors must record debt-related calls and, on request, give you a copy within ten days at no charge. A caller who has mentioned jail is a caller whose recording you may want.

## What to Do Instead of Worrying

1. **Open the mail.** The costly outcomes in this article all begin with a document that was not read.
2. **Diarise every deadline** — 20 days to file a defence in Ontario, 30 days' notice of an examination.
3. **Attend anything with a court file number**, even if you cannot pay a cent. Attending is free; not attending is what creates risk.
4. **Ask for the extended payment plan** if you have taken three payday loans in 63 days in Ontario — instalments, prepayable, no penalty.
5. **Get free help.** Community legal clinics and non-profit credit counselling agencies cost nothing and deal with this weekly.

**Can you go to jail for not paying a payday loan Canada** borrowers took out? No — debt itself is civil, and the law says so in as many words.
