**Average rent Canada 2026** figures show a market pulling in two directions: the average asking rent for all property types was $2,033 in June, down 4.3% year over year, yet up 0.2% from May. That is according to the Rentals.ca and Urbanation National Rent Report published July 8, 2026, on June data. The annual drop marks the 21st consecutive month of decline — genuine relief for renters — but the third straight monthly increase is a reminder that costs are quietly creeping back up.

## What the Average Rent Canada 2026 Report Shows

The headline is a study in mixed signals. Measured against last year, rents are still easing; measured against last month, they are edging higher on seasonal demand. Here is the snapshot from the report:

| Segment | June 2026 asking rent | Year-over-year change |
|---|---|---|
| All property types (national average) | $2,033 | −4.3% |
| Purpose-built apartments | $2,034 | −3.1% |
| Houses / townhomes | $2,017 | −7.4% |
| Nova Scotia (most expensive province) | $2,360 | — |
| British Columbia | $2,347 | — |

The year-over-year decline is the 21st in a row, a long stretch of gradual cooling after the sharp run-up of recent years. But the month-over-month figure — up 0.2% — is the third consecutive monthly rise, driven by the usual spring and summer surge in demand as leases turn over and students and movers hit the market.

## Relief Year Over Year, Pressure Month to Month

For renters, the practical read is that the worst of the spike has passed, but the relief is modest and may be fading at the margin. A 4.3% annual drop on a $2,033 average works out to real savings compared with a year ago — yet if you are signing a new lease this summer, you are competing in a market that has just risen three months running.

Property type matters more than usual right now. Purpose-built apartments proved the most resilient, easing only 3.1% to about $2,034, while houses and townhomes fell the steepest, down 7.4% to about $2,017. In other words, the biggest bargains relative to last year are in ground-oriented rentals, not apartments.

There is also a possible turning point worth watching. In Toronto, rents rose 1.2% from May and were down only 1.9% year over year — a much shallower annual decline than the national figure, which some analysts read as an early sign of the market stabilizing in the country's largest rental city.

## What It Means for Your Budget

Softer annual rents are good news, but they do not change the core rule of renting affordably: budget from today's price, not last year's. With monthly asking rents ticking up again, plan around the number on the lease in front of you and leave room for utilities, insurance and the rest of the cost of living, which is still rising.

A few steps help renters stay ahead in this market:

- **Build the housing cost around your income, not the ceiling a landlord will approve.** Passing an affordability check is not the same as the payment being comfortable.
- **Know how a new obligation lands.** Before adding a car payment or other borrowing on top of rent, run your [debt-to-income ratio](/resources/guides/understanding-debt-to-income-ratio) so you can see how much monthly room you actually have.
- **Protect your rental applications.** In a firmer market, landlords lean on credit checks — knowing what is in your file first can make the difference. Our guide to [understanding your credit reports](/resources/guides/understanding-credit-reports) walks through it.
- **Do not fund routine rent with high-interest debt.** Relying on credit to cover a recurring bill is a warning sign that the housing cost is too high for the budget, not a short-term fix.

If a genuine one-off gap appears — a move, a deposit, a broken essential — borrow deliberately rather than reflexively. ## The Bottom Line

The June **average rent Canada 2026** picture is one of easing pressure that has not disappeared: $2,033 nationally, down 4.3% on the year for a 21st straight month, but up for a third month in a row as demand returns. Nova Scotia and B.C. remain the priciest provinces, houses and townhomes offer the biggest annual relief, and Toronto may be steadying. For renters, the takeaway is simple — take the year-over-year relief where you can find it, budget from this month's number, and keep debt out of your regular rent cheque.
