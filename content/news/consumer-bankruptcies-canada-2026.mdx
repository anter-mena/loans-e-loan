**Consumer bankruptcies Canada 2026** data took a sharp turn in the second quarter: filings rose to **8,600 from 7,576** in the first quarter, an increase of roughly **13.5%**, even as consumer proposals declined. The Office of the Superintendent of Bankruptcy (OSB) released the figures on 10 August. The overall insolvency headline barely moved — but the mix beneath it moved a great deal, and that is where the more useful information sits.

![Consumer bankruptcies Canada 2026 — an advisor helping a client review debt options on a laptop](https://images.pexels.com/photos/3823488/pexels-photo-3823488.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## Summary

- **8,600 consumer bankruptcies** in Q2 2026, up about **13.5%** from Q1's 7,576
- **28,923 consumer proposals**, down about **2.1%** from Q1's 29,545
- Total consumer insolvencies of **37,523**, up just **1.1%** on the quarter and **6.9%** year over year
- The quarter was the heaviest for consumer filings since **2009**

## Consumer Bankruptcies Canada 2026: The Number Behind the Number

Most coverage of the OSB release led with the total. At 37,523 consumer filings, Q2 2026 was up only 1.1% on the previous quarter — a figure that reads like a plateau.

The composition tells a different story:

| Filing type | Q1 2026 | Q2 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer bankruptcies | 7,576 | **8,600** | **+13.5%** |
| Consumer proposals | 29,545 | **28,923** | **−2.1%** |
| **Total consumer** | 37,121 | **37,523** | +1.1% |

Roughly a thousand filings moved from one column to the other. The consumer bankruptcies Canada 2026 count rose by more in one quarter than the total insolvency figure did — because the proposal count fell at the same time.

## Why the Mix Matters More Than the Total

The two insolvency routes counted in the consumer bankruptcies Canada 2026 data are not interchangeable, and the difference is essentially about affordability.

A **consumer proposal** is a legally binding agreement, filed through a Licensed Insolvency Trustee, to repay a portion of what is owed over a period of up to five years. It requires the filer to sustain agreed monthly payments for that period. In exchange, the filer generally keeps their assets and the credit impact is lighter.

A **bankruptcy** discharges most unsecured debts without that multi-year payment commitment, but can involve surrendering certain assets and carries a heavier and longer credit consequence.

So a proposal is, in a real sense, the option available to people who still have some room in their budget. When the balance tips toward bankruptcy while the total holds steady, the most straightforward reading is that a growing share of filers no longer have that room.

## Background: A Plateau at a High Altitude

The consumer bankruptcies Canada 2026 totals sit inside a wider picture: Canadian insolvency filings have been running at levels not seen since the financial crisis. The 2009 comparison has now been used for consecutive quarters — the Q1 2026 total of 37,121 was described the same way when it landed, as our earlier report on [Canadian consumer debt and insolvencies](/news/canadian-consumer-debt-insolvencies-2026) noted.

Charles St-Arnaud, chief economist at Servus Credit Union, told BNN Bloomberg that "high household debt and stagnating purchasing power have put pressure on Canadians' finances in recent years," adding that insolvency rates "appear to be stabilizing near levels last seen in 2009."

That framing is fair on the totals. It is worth holding alongside the compositional shift, which suggests the stabilisation is not evenly distributed across filers.

## Why This Matters for Borrowers

The practical lesson in the consumer bankruptcies Canada 2026 data is about **timing**, not about panic.

1. **The cheaper option has an entry requirement.** Because a proposal depends on sustainable payments, it is most available to households that act before their budget is fully consumed by minimum payments and fees.
2. **Free advice comes first.** A Licensed Insolvency Trustee offers a free initial consultation and is the only professional who can file either process in Canada. Non-profit credit counselling agencies help with budgeting at little or no cost.
3. **Neither outcome is permanent.** A proposal typically stays on a credit file about three years after completion; a first bankruptcy six to seven years after discharge. Rebuilding usually starts with a secured card or a small reporting loan.
4. **Watch your own leading indicators.** Missing a payment, using credit for groceries, or borrowing to service existing debt are earlier signals than any national statistic.

## Consumer Bankruptcies Canada 2026: Key Takeaways

- Consumer bankruptcies rose **13.5% quarter over quarter** while proposals slipped **2.1%**
- The total consumer insolvency figure moved just **1.1%**, masking that shift entirely
- Q2 2026 was the heaviest quarter for consumer filings since **2009**
- The shift is consistent with filers arriving with **less budget room**, not simply more filers
- Acting earlier keeps the lower-impact option on the table

## Conclusion

Headline insolvency totals are a blunt instrument. The consumer bankruptcies Canada 2026 figures for the second quarter show a national total that barely moved and an underlying mix that moved meaningfully — with about a thousand filings shifting from the option that requires affordable payments to the one that does not. For households under pressure, that is an argument for having the conversation sooner rather than later, while the full range of options is still open.
