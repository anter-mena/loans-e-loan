**Published August 19, 2026**

## Brief summary

**Canada housing completions 2026** data for July, published by Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation on **August 18**, rose **8.1%** month over month to **19,773 units** in centres of 50,000 or more — in the same release that showed housing starts falling **5%**. Units under construction reached **373,091**, while **141,480** approved units remained unstarted.

![Canada housing completions 2026 — modern apartment buildings finished and ready for occupancy, the output end of the construction pipeline](https://images.pexels.com/photos/323780/pexels-photo-323780.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## Introduction

Most coverage of the August 18 release led with the 5% drop in housing starts. That is the correct headline, but it is only half the report — and the other half moved the opposite way.

When a headline number falls, the more useful question is whether the composition beneath it moved too. In July, it did.

## The main story: two ends of one pipeline

CMHC reports several construction measures in the same release, and in July they split cleanly in two directions:

| Measure | July 2026 | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| **Completions**, centres 50,000+ | **19,773 units** | **▲ 8.1%** from June |
| **Units under construction**, centres 50,000+ | **373,091 units** | ▲ 0.6% month over month |
| **Approved units not yet started** | **141,480 units** | ▲ 3% month over month |
| Housing starts, seasonally adjusted annual rate | 229,074 units | ▼ 5% from June |
| Actual starts, centres 10,000+ | 18,834 units | ▼ 19% year over year |

Everything measuring **work already underway or finished** rose. Everything measuring **new work beginning** fell.

**Tania Bourassa-Ochoa, Deputy Chief Economist at CMHC**, framed the release as continuity rather than a break, saying "July's results show housing starts continuing to moderate as new home construction evolves per CMHC's recent Summer Update."

### Why Canada housing completions 2026 and starts can move opposite ways

The two figures describe decisions made years apart.

A completion recorded in July 2026 reflects a project that broke ground in roughly 2023 or 2024, under the financing conditions and construction costs of that period. A start recorded in July 2026 reflects a decision made this summer.

So a month where **Canada housing completions 2026** climb while starts fall is not a contradiction. It is a pipeline emptying faster than it is being refilled.

## Background: how Canada housing completions 2026 are measured

CMHC publishes this data monthly, and the agency's own **Housing Market Outlook Summer Update**, released **July 22, 2026**, had already forecast weakness. It projected housing starts continuing to decline across the forecast period on weak demand, elevated inventories and high construction costs, with home prices falling in 2026 before returning to modest growth in 2027 and 2028.

**Kevin Hughes, Deputy Chief Economist at CMHC**, said in that update: "Price reductions have not yet been enough to bring demand back into the market as economic uncertainty, income growth and borrowing conditions all have played a role in sidelining buyers."

The July figures are consistent with that forecast rather than a departure from it.

## Why Canada housing completions 2026 matter for rents

The backlog is the number to watch, and it is the reason completions deserve attention alongside the starts headline.

**141,480 units hold building permits but have not broken ground**, and that figure rose 3% in a single month. Those projects have cleared the planning and approval hurdles that usually take the longest. What is holding them is something else — financing costs, construction costs, or a builder's judgement that the numbers do not work at current prices.

That distinction matters because it is reversible in a way that a planning bottleneck is not. A backlog waiting on economics can move quickly if borrowing costs ease. A backlog waiting on approvals cannot.

For households, three practical points:

- **Nothing here changes housing costs this year.** Completions arriving now were started years ago.
- **Near-term supply is intact.** With 373,091 units under construction, deliveries continue through 2027 and 2028.
- **The late-decade picture is where the risk sits.** Fewer starts today means fewer completions around 2029, and rental supply is the channel through which that eventually reaches renters.

## Key takeaways

- Completions **19,773 units**, up **8.1%** from June
- Units under construction **373,091**, up 0.6%
- Approved but unstarted units **141,480**, up 3% in one month
- Starts down 5% monthly and 19% year over year on actual starts
- The trend measure fell only **0.5%**, indicating moderation rather than a stop
- Everything already in progress rose; only new beginnings fell

## Conclusion

July was not a bad month for Canadian home construction so much as a **divided** one. Builders finished more homes than in June and are still working through the largest inventory of under-construction units in years. What they did less of was start new projects.

Read on its own, the 5% headline suggests a market losing momentum. Read alongside **Canada housing completions 2026** and the 141,480-unit unstarted backlog, it suggests something more specific: an industry completing what it has already committed to, while waiting for the economics to justify committing to more.
