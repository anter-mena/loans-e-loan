Canada student loans 2026 come with a rare run of good news for students heading back to school: the federal portion stays interest-free, non-repayable grants are 40% larger, and the weekly loan limit holds at $300 — a package the government says will help about 571,000 students this academic year. If you are financing school this fall, the practical takeaway is simple. Federal aid is cheaper and more forgiving than it has been in years, so it should be your first stop, well ahead of any private or short-term loan.

## What Canada Student Loans 2026 Give Students

The centrepiece is cost. Federal student loans in Canada have been interest-free since April 1, 2023, a change Ottawa later made permanent. Employment and Social Development Canada estimates the move saves the average borrower about **$350 in 2026** alone — money that used to disappear into interest.

For the 2026–27 academic year, the government extended a set of enhanced supports. Non-repayable **Canada Student Grants rise by 40%**, and the **Canada Student Loan weekly limit stays at $300**, up from the pre-pandemic $210. Roughly **571,000 students** are expected to benefit, according to the federal announcement.

The one-line read on Canada student loans 2026: borrowing federally costs you nothing in interest, the free-money portion is bigger, and you can access more per week if you need it.

## Repayment Waits — and the RAP Backstops You

Repayment on federal loans does not begin until **six months after you leave school**, and even then there is a safety net. The **Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP)** can reduce or fully pause monthly payments based on income. Under the program, the Government of Canada says borrowers earning **less than $40,000 a year** can qualify to make no payments at all while they get established.

That combination — interest-free borrowing, a six-month grace period, and income-tested relief — is exactly why federal aid should be exhausted before you reach for anything costlier.

## How to Borrow Smart for School

Federal grants and loans rarely cover every dollar, and that is where students get into trouble. When a gap opens up in August, the temptation is to grab whatever is fastest — a payday loan, a buy-now-pay-later plan, or a high-rate private line. Those are almost always the most expensive money you can borrow, and none of them come with a 0% rate or a RAP-style pause if your income dips.

E-Loan is a personal-loan platform, not a source of student aid, and we will say it plainly: max out federal help first. If a genuine, unavoidable gap remains and you are an adult with steady income, a regulated, fixed-rate [personal loan](/loans/by-type/personal-loans) is at least more transparent than a payday loan — one balance, one rate, one predictable payment. Before you borrow a dollar, it pays to understand [your debt-to-income ratio](/resources/guides/understanding-debt-to-income-ratio) and to check [your credit report](/resources/guides/understanding-credit-reports), because both drive the rate you are offered. When you are ready to weigh real options, you can [start an application](/application-form) and see terms before you commit — but only for gaps federal aid genuinely cannot fill.

## The Bottom Line

For once, the headline on Canada student loans 2026 is genuinely encouraging: interest-free federal borrowing, 40% larger grants, a $300 weekly limit, and a six-month grace period backed by the Repayment Assistance Plan for about 571,000 students. The smart move this back-to-school season is to lean on that support first, borrow federally before privately, and treat any personal loan as a last resort for a gap you truly cannot close — never as a substitute for aid that is cheaper, more flexible, and, right now, interest-free.
