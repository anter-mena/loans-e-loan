**Canada housing starts 2026** are running below last year: builders began 20,265 homes in June 2026 across centres of 10,000 or more people, down 13% from 23,292 a year earlier, according to the Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC). Year-to-date starts reached 113,017 units, a 1% dip from the same stretch of 2025. Completions rose and homes under construction held roughly steady, but CMHC expects full-year starts to land below 2025 as costs climb and demand softens. Here is what a slower building pace could mean for future supply, prices and renters.

> **Verified July 30, 2026:** All figures below are from CMHC's June 2026 housing starts release. National totals can move differently from any single city, property type or neighbourhood.

## Canada Housing Starts 2026: The Numbers

The June report is a picture of a construction sector easing off its pace. Fewer homes broke ground than a year earlier, and the year-to-date tally slipped as well — modestly at the national level, but enough to keep 2026 tracking under 2025. Here is the CMHC snapshot for June:

| Metric (June 2026, CMHC) | Figure | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Housing starts, centres 10,000+ | **20,265 units** | −13% vs June 2025 |
| Year-to-date starts (Jan–Jun) | **113,017 units** | −1% vs 2025 |
| Homes under construction, centres 50,000+ | **~375,469 units** | +0.2% month over month |
| Completions | **18,298 units** | +8.4% |

Two of those lines are worth sitting with. First, the 13% drop in monthly starts is the headline: it is a clear step down from June 2025's 23,292 units. Second, the pipeline of homes already **under construction** was essentially flat, edging up just 0.2% month over month to about 375,469 units. In plain terms, work already begun is still moving through, but fewer new projects are joining the queue behind it. Completions, meanwhile, rose 8.4% to 18,298 — homes finishing now reflect decisions made months or years ago, not today's slower start rate.

## Where Starts Rose and Fell

The national figure hides a sharp regional split. CMHC's data shows two of Canada's largest markets building more than a year ago, while the third pulled back hard:

| Major market | Year-over-year starts | CMHC's note |
|---|---|---|
| Toronto | **+25%** | Higher multi-unit construction |
| Montreal | **+10%** | Higher multi-unit construction |
| Vancouver | **−35%** | Lower multi-unit and single-detached |

Toronto and Montreal both leaned on **multi-unit** projects — apartments and condominiums — to post year-over-year gains, a reminder that most new urban housing now arrives in stacked form rather than as single-detached houses. Vancouver moved the other way, with CMHC citing weaker multi-unit *and* single-detached activity for its 35% decline. Because a handful of large multi-unit projects can swing a city's monthly number, these figures can be volatile from month to month; the direction matters more than any single reading.

## Why Construction Is Slowing

The Canada housing starts 2026 trend is not the product of a single roadblock. CMHC frames the year as one of softer building, and it expects full-year starts to finish below 2025. The agency points to a cluster of pressures rather than any single cause:

- **Rising uncertainty.** A cloudier economic outlook makes builders and lenders more cautious about committing to large, multi-year projects.
- **Higher development costs.** Land, labour, materials and financing have all grown more expensive, squeezing the math on new projects.
- **Weaker demand.** Softer buyer appetite makes it harder to pre-sell the units that many projects need before breaking ground.
- **More unsold homes.** A larger inventory of completed-but-unsold homes gives developers a reason to wait rather than add new supply.

None of these is a crisis on its own. Together, though, they raise the bar a project has to clear to get started — which is how a 13% monthly drop and a below-2025 forecast emerge from the same set of conditions.

## Why It Matters for Renters and Buyers

Housing starts are a leading indicator: they measure homes begun today that will not be lived in for months or years. So a slower 2026 is less about this summer's rents and more about the supply pipeline further out. When fewer homes enter that pipeline while the population keeps growing, the gap between supply and demand can widen — which, all else equal, tends to support both rents and prices down the road. All else is rarely equal, of course; interest rates, immigration, incomes and local zoning all pull on the outcome too.

For renters and would-be buyers, the practical takeaway is planning, not panic. The Canada housing starts 2026 slowdown is a supply-side story that plays out over years, so it is a reason to budget from today's costs and keep your finances resilient, not to rush a decision. ## Key Takeaways

- **Starts fell 13% in June.** CMHC recorded 20,265 housing starts in centres of 10,000+, down from 23,292 in June 2025.
- **The year is tracking lower.** Year-to-date starts of 113,017 units are down 1%, and CMHC expects full-year 2026 to finish below 2025.
- **The pipeline held steady.** Homes under construction were roughly flat (+0.2% month over month), and completions rose 8.4% to 18,298.
- **Cities diverged.** Toronto (+25%) and Montreal (+10%) built more on multi-unit strength; Vancouver fell 35%.
- **The impact is long-term.** Fewer starts now can tighten future supply, so plan your budget for resilience rather than reacting to a single month.

## The Bottom Line

The **Canada housing starts 2026** figures describe a construction sector building at a slower pace than last year — down 13% in June, down 1% year to date, and forecast by CMHC to close the year below 2025. Beneath that national number sits a real regional split, with Toronto and Montreal up on multi-unit projects and Vancouver sharply lower. For households, the signal is patient rather than urgent: a thinner supply pipeline is a multi-year story, best met with a budget built on today's numbers and a cautious approach to new debt.
